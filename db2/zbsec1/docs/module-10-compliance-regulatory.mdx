# Module 10: Banking compliance and regulatory control mapping

**Recommended time:** 4 hours

<CourseProgress compact />

Compliance work fails when teams treat every document as the same kind of requirement. A bank may be subject to legislation, regulator-issued standards, industry/payment-card obligations, contractual commitments and voluntary security frameworks at the same time. Your task is to determine **scope, control objective, technical implementation, accountable owner and evidence**.

:::info Educational mapping only
This module is not legal advice. Applicability, interpretation, reporting obligations and evidence retention must be validated by the institution's legal, compliance, privacy, risk and payment-card governance functions.
:::

## 10.1 Classify the source before mapping it

| Source | Type | How to use it in this course |
|---|---|---|
| POPIA | South African privacy law | Identify security safeguards and security-compromise obligations for personal information processing |
| Prudential Authority / FSCA Joint Standards | Financial-sector regulatory standards for institutions in scope | Map IT governance, cybersecurity and cyber-resilience controls to accountable owners/evidence |
| PCI DSS v4.0.1 | Payment-card industry standard/contractual ecosystem requirement | Apply to the cardholder-data environment when the organisation is in scope |
| NIST CSF 2.0 | Cybersecurity risk-management framework | Organise governance, identify/protect/detect/respond/recover outcomes |
| OWASP guidance | Application-security guidance | Translate common application/database risks into engineering controls and tests |

A requirement can be technically similar across sources but still have different scope, evidence and accountability.

## 10.2 South African financial-sector standards

### Joint Standard 1 of 2023 — IT Governance and Risk Management

For institutions in scope, use the standard to drive questions such as:

- Is IT risk governance owned and approved at the correct level?
- Are critical Db2 services identified and classified?
- Are access, change, resilience and third-party risks formally controlled?
- Is there adequate oversight of outsourced/platform services?
- Is control effectiveness monitored and reported?

Db2 evidence may include:

- subsystem/application ownership;
- access-control matrices;
- change/release records for packages/security configuration;
- vulnerability/maintenance process;
- recovery test evidence;
- exception/risk acceptance records.

### Joint Standard 2 of 2024 — Cybersecurity and Cyber Resilience

The Joint Standard establishes cybersecurity and cyber-resilience requirements for financial institutions in scope and took effect **1 June 2025** under the associated commencement notice.

For Db2, translate the standard into control themes such as:

- governance and accountability;
- asset/data classification;
- identity and privileged access;
- secure configuration and vulnerability management;
- network security;
- data protection/cryptography;
- monitoring/detection;
- incident response;
- recovery and resilience;
- testing and continuous improvement.

Do not create a “Db2 compliance silo”. The Db2 control must connect to the institution's broader cyber-risk framework and critical business services.

## 10.3 POPIA — security safeguards

POPIA Section 19 requires responsible parties to establish and maintain appropriate, reasonable technical and organisational measures to protect personal information, including identifying foreseeable risks, establishing safeguards, verifying that safeguards are effectively implemented and keeping them updated as risks evolve.

For a Db2 banking environment, evidence could include:

- personal-data classification;
- least-privilege access and periodic recertification;
- row/column controls;
- encryption and key governance;
- logging/monitoring;
- vulnerability/maintenance processes;
- secure disposal/retention controls;
- incident and security-compromise response procedures.

Sections 20–22 address operator/security-compromise considerations. Your privacy/legal team must determine exactly how incident notification obligations apply to a specific event.

## 10.4 PCI DSS v4.0.1

PCI DSS v4.0.1 is the current PCI DSS revision in this course baseline. It applies to the cardholder-data environment according to PCI SSC scoping rules and the organisation's acquiring/payment relationships.

Do **not** assume mainframe storage is exempt. PCI SSC guidance explicitly addresses PCI DSS encryption requirements in mainframe environments storing cardholder data.

Db2-relevant PCI control themes include:

- restrict access by business need to know;
- identify/authenticate users and non-human accounts;
- protect cardholder data at rest and in transit;
- manage cryptographic keys;
- secure application development and prevent injection;
- log and monitor access to cardholder data and privileged functions;
- vulnerability/change management;
- regular control testing;
- incident response.

### Cardholder-data minimisation

If the application does not need full PAN, do not retrieve full PAN and hide it later in the UI. Prefer data minimisation close to the source through approved views/masks/tokenisation/role policy.

## 10.5 NIST CSF 2.0 as an organising framework

NIST CSF 2.0 can organise Db2 security outcomes under six functions:

```mermaid
flowchart LR
  G[GOVERN] --> I[IDENTIFY]
  I --> P[PROTECT]
  P --> D[DETECT]
  D --> R[RESPOND]
  R --> C[RECOVER]
  C --> G
```

Example mapping:

| Function | ZBSEC1 controls |
|---|---|
| GOVERN | policy, ownership, SoD, regulatory mapping, risk acceptance |
| IDENTIFY | data classification, subsystem/application inventory, threat model |
| PROTECT | RACF, Db2 authorities, packages, TLS, RCAC, cryptography |
| DETECT | audit policies, IFCIDs, SMF, SIEM, anomaly detection |
| RESPOND | identity containment, evidence preservation, playbooks, notification decision process |
| RECOVER | credential/key recovery, Db2 restore/failover, control revalidation |

NIST CSF is a risk framework, not a substitute for an applicable law or regulator standard.

## 10.6 Build a control-to-evidence matrix

A compliance matrix should not stop at “control implemented”.

| Requirement / objective | Db2 control | Prevent / detect / recover | Test | Evidence | Owner | Frequency |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Restrict sensitive data to business need | role + package + row/column control | Prevent | positive + negative persona test | catalog extract + test record | Data owner / SECADM | release + recertification |
| Protect distributed credentials/data | DDF + AT-TLS + auth policy | Prevent | TLS/auth negative test | TLS evidence + DDF + RACF records | Network/IAM/DBA | continuous + change |
| Monitor privileged activity | Db2 audit + SMF + SIEM | Detect | generate known test event | raw event + alert/case | SOC | continuous |
| Recover encrypted service | DFSMS + ICSF + DR key availability | Recover | end-to-end DR exercise | recovery log + application validation | DR/Crypto/DBA | risk-based schedule |

## 10.7 Evidence quality rubric

Score each control 0–4:

- **0 — assertion only:** “We do this.”
- **1 — configuration screenshot:** evidence exists but scope/ownership unclear.
- **2 — documented configuration + owner:** design is traceable.
- **3 — tested control:** positive/negative or recovery/detection test proves operation.
- **4 — continuously governed:** tested, monitored, recertified, exceptions tracked and evidence retained.

A mature bank should aim to move critical controls from **configured** to **proven and governed**.

## 10.8 Lab: build a mini banking compliance pack

Choose one fictional scope: **mobile-banking customer data** or **card-payment authorisation**.

Create at least 20 rows with these fields:

```text
Source / requirement identifier
Control objective
Applicability rationale
Db2/z/OS control
Other platform control
Owner
Test method
Evidence source
Frequency
Known gap
Compensating control / remediation
Risk acceptance owner
```

Include at least:

- one Joint Standard 1/2023 governance requirement;
- five Joint Standard 2/2024 cyber-resilience themes;
- four POPIA security-safeguard/privacy rows;
- six PCI DSS rows if you chose cardholder data;
- NIST CSF function mapping for each control.
