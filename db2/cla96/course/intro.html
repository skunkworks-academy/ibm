Orientation: learn as a production DBA

This self-paced learning companion turns the public CLA96G skill scope into an original, practice-led DBA pathway. The target is not command memorisation. The target is repeatable operational judgment: understand the layer you are changing, collect evidence, make the smallest justified intervention, validate the result, and leave an audit trail.

Course alignment

IBM currently describes CLA96G as IBM Db2 12: Foundation for Relational DBAs, focused on Db2 12.1 administration, utilities, recovery, security, concurrency, monitoring and performance. This independent companion aligns to those public objectives without reproducing IBM courseware.

What you should be able to do at the end​

By completion, you should be able to:

  • explain the relationship between the operating system, Db2 instance, database, schemas and database objects;
  • identify the exact Db2 service level and reason about maintenance scope before changing an environment;
  • inspect and change database configuration using a baseline/change/validation workflow;
  • operate data movement and maintenance utilities while monitoring their state and table implications;
  • design and test backup, restore and rollforward procedures against recovery objectives;
  • diagnose locks, timeouts, deadlocks and isolation-related concurrency behavior;
  • separate authentication, authorization, task authorities, roles and object privileges;
  • reason about RCAC, LBAC, trusted contexts, auditing and encryption as layered controls;
  • use RUNSTATS, indexes, EXPLAIN and monitoring data to build a performance hypothesis;
  • evaluate advisor and AI-assisted recommendations without bypassing change governance.

Recommended learning environment​

A real lab is strongly recommended. The browser terminal in this course is intentionally a simulator and does not connect to Db2.

Db2 environment

An isolated Db2 12.1 lab or an organization-approved sandbox. Never test destructive recovery or privilege changes on production first.

DBA notebook

Record timestamps, commands, outputs, screenshots, hypotheses, decisions and rollback points. Treat evidence as part of the exercise.

Sample workload

Use a database such as SAMPLE or a disposable training database with enough data to observe utility, locking and plan behavior.

The five-step operating discipline​

01 · BaselineCapture service level, configuration, workload rate, health and symptoms before intervention.
02 · HypothesisName the mechanism you believe explains the symptom and what evidence would support or falsify it.
03 · ChangeChoose the smallest scoped change that tests the hypothesis and preserve rollback values.
04 · ValidateCompare post-change technical signals and user-facing outcomes with the baseline.
05 · DocumentRecord what changed, why, who approved it, evidence, outcome and follow-up.

This sequence will appear repeatedly. It is deliberately more important than any individual command.

How progress and assessment work​

  • Each major milestone has a Mark complete control.
  • Progress is stored in your browser; no identity or personal data is required.
  • If the content is launched from a SCORM 1.2 LMS, the course attempts to report lesson status and final score.
  • The final assessment uses 16 scenario-based questions and a 75% mastery threshold.
  • The completion record is evidence of this companion’s assessment only; it is not an IBM certification or an official IBM course completion record.

Reference discipline​

Prefer vendor documentation for exact command syntax, parameter semantics, supported configurations and service-level behavior. The course intentionally teaches a decision model and then points you back to authoritative reference material.
